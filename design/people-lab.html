Lab 08 · the connective layer

People

people.md opens by refusing a category: "People is not a CRM. It is a lightweight entity that connects the other modules." And its first invariant is stronger than that — "A Person is a layer, not an atom. It does not exist on its own; it classifies links."

Everything that follows is measured against that sentence, and one thing in the module contradicts it. The strongest thing in the module — the prep card, which the doc calls "the highest-value feature" — depends on it completely.

A layer, not an atom

"A Person is a layer, not an atom. It does not exist on its own; it classifies links." That is the module's first invariant, and it is what makes the prep card possible: "every field of it comes out of the People graph… The card needs no store of its own and adds no data."

Three other parts of the same doc treat a Person as an atom: a + that creates one with no links, an empty state that explains arrival by linking, and archival that removes one from every list. The claim is the module's foundation and the doc's own affordances contradict it.

cut the tell · a rule the model doesn't enforce isn't one

Rewritten:

"A Person has no content of its own. It carries a name, a source, and a lifecycle — no tasks, no events, no notes. Everything the People module shows about a person is read from the links that point at them."

That is checkable against object-model.md, and it is what the module actually relies on.

Changed
Replaces the "layer, not an atom / does not exist on its own" claim with a statement about data rather than existence — which is testable, and which the prep card's design already depends on.
Cost
It loses the sentence that makes the module's idea memorable, and it weakens the strongest argument against building CRM features into People. "Does not exist on its own" is doing rhetorical work that a data statement does not.
Against rules
One: rewriting an invariant is an ADR-shaped change in this repo, and the invariant is cited from object-model.md. The honest version keeps the sentence and lets the affordances be its exceptions, listed.
Recommended

Keep the invariant, and let it say what a Person may not have.

the claim is about content, and it is already true

The invariant, with its boundary stated:

"A Person is a layer, not an atom — it classifies links and holds no content of its own. It may exist before it has links (+), because a name is not content, and it may be archived like any other entity. What it may never have is a field no link can produce."

Which is exactly what the prep card proves. Its fields:

attendees last met last note last completed task open tasks

Five fields from three edges, and the card stores nothing.

Changed
The invariant keeps its first half and gains its boundary. The +, the empty state's explanation, and archival stop contradicting it — and the prep card's claim becomes the test: a Person has no field that a link cannot produce. That is the sentence that stops People becoming a CRM, and unlike "does not exist on its own" it can be enforced.
Cost
It makes the invariant longer and less quotable, and it formally permits the unlinked Person — so the module gains a state with no designed surface, which the row screen shows a Person's [last] slot cannot express.
Still needs
What an unlinked Person's row shows. The row has three parts and two of them are derived from links, so a Person with no links has nothing to fill them — which is a drawing problem the doc created by asserting an invariant the rest of the doc ignores. Either the invariant is firmed up (no unlinked People, and the + goes) or the row gains a state. The lab can show both; the choice is about whether People is a place you add people to.

Contacts import

Cost

Recommended

size the write, then make it reversible in one step

Three additions, all definitional:

1 State the rule. Match on phone, then email, then exact name — and a match links, never creates.
2 State the count before the write, on the button.
3 One import, one undo. The import is a set; archiving it is one action.

Changed
Three silences become statements, and one of them (the matching rule) is the difference between an import that links and an import that duplicates. The undo needs a batch identity on person.created — an ADR, and the only structural change here.
Cost
A batch id is the app's first event field that exists purely to support a reversal, and it is the thin end of a wedge: every bulk operation afterwards will want one. It also makes archival two-tier — archived alone, or archived as part of an import — which person.unarchived does not distinguish.
Still needs
Whether a contact is a Person. Import creates People from a phone's address book, which is a list of contact records — and the doc's first line says "People is not a CRM". A 312-person import makes the People list a contact list with a `[last: —]` on most rows, which is the thing the module refuses to be. The honest options are to import only contacts the user has interacted with (needs data the app does not have) or to accept that People starts as a contact list and becomes a graph.
