Calendar lab

The Calendar is the app's default mode and the hub every other module attaches to — the surface where the Day becomes visible. Each view is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left, and the same three lines on the right — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks.

The fourth row is the recommended one: the synthesis, not a fifth option.

fixed: token set · palette · type scale · Lucide · row heights · motion rules · `calendar.md`'s eight sections

Day view — the composition, at last

`calendar.md` specifies the Day view as a fixed vertical composition of eight parts: day header, now line, timeline, habits due, metric log, day notes, top three, completed. Seven of those eight have a height that does not bend — the timeline is the only one that can flex — and three of them are lists of `row-default` rows.

Nobody has ever seen the eight stacked. v1 below is the measurement, and it is why this view came first: if the composition does not fit, then every other Calendar view is being designed on top of a problem.

Baseline

All eight sections, in the specified order, at the specified heights.

The measurement

Week view — one sentence, and it is a hard one

`calendar.md` gives the Day view eight numbered parts, its token decisions, and a measurement. It gives the Week this, in full:

“Week view — 7 days side by side. Events only; tasks and habits collapse to counts.”

One sentence of layout. Everything else — the axis, its window, whether a title is ever drawn, what “collapse to counts” counts, where the now line goes, what a bare week looks like — is undecided. So this view is not being checked against a layout. It is being asked whether the sentence can be drawn at all.

It can. But not the way the Day view works, and the measurement below is why.

Baseline

“7 days side by side”, read literally: seven equal columns, every event in the column it falls in, with its title.

The axes swap. Days become rows, the clock becomes a horizontal track, and every block gets the whole width of the phone to be read in. The trailing number is the day's load, so the counts survive without a separate strip.
Cost
It does not work, and the measurement says so. Ten hours across a track is far less than the phone is wide, so a 30-minute event ends up narrower than the column it was meant to replace. Rotation moves the constraint; it does not remove it. And with the days stacked, two days can no longer be compared by eye — which was the one thing the columns were for.
Against rules
Two. “7 days side by side” is explicit, and `motion-vocabulary.md` requires that during a calendar zoom “events hold their visual position” — a 90° rotation is the opposite of holding position.
Design taste

Delete the axis. A week is seven days, and a day is a list of what is on it.

Takes
Baseline for seven columns and one shared gutter — the sentence is right, it is simply not a layout. Impeccable for the realisation that proportionality and legibility are the same decision, so the Week earns its chart by giving up the names. Frontend design for testing rotation and proving the constraint is the clock rather than the column count. Design taste for showing what deleting the axis produces: the Day view, seven times, in the place where the Day already works.
Fixes
The Week becomes a chart with a two-step way in. Tap a column and it outlines while the focus line names that day and its load; tap the focus line and the day opens. One tap cannot both pick a column out and leave the week, so it does not try. The selection starts on today, which is already the tinted and accented column. The all-day band spans Thu–Fri — an item covering two days, which no other Calendar surface can draw whole. The counts strip spends “collapse to counts” on telling the days apart rather than on naming anything, and the now rule crosses all seven columns but belongs to none of them.
Still needs
Five decisions this view cannot make for itself. One: `calendar.md`'s Scope gives the Week a sentence and the Day eight numbered parts — the Week needs a layout section of its own, or this row stands in for one. Two: that doc's Surfaces section lists five surfaces and omits Week, Month and Year, all three of which `03-experience/surfaces.md` lists as screens. Three: “The Calendar has no whole-view empty state” is false for the Week, and the reason given for it — “the Daily Note always exists” — is a Day-view argument. A week with nothing on it is genuinely bare; Sunday's column above is that case in every variant. Four: `06-flows/doing-the-day.md` ties the now line to “the calendar day view and the header of other modes”. The Week is neither, so it has no specified now line, while every variant here needs one and this one draws it as a rule. Five: the axis window is unspecified — a fixed clock range (this mock uses 08:00–18:00) or one derived from the week's own earliest and latest events. It decides whether two weeks can be compared at a glance, which is the view's entire purpose.
