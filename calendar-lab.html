Calendar lab

The Calendar is the app's default mode and the hub every other module attaches to — the surface where the Day becomes visible. Each view is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left, and the same three lines on the right — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks.

The fourth row is the recommended one: the synthesis, not a fifth option.

fixed: token set · palette · type scale · Lucide · row heights · motion rules · `calendar.md`'s eight sections

Day view — the composition, at last

`calendar.md` specifies the Day view as a fixed vertical composition of eight parts: day header, now line, timeline, habits due, metric log, day notes, top three, completed. Seven of those eight have a height that does not bend — the timeline is the only one that can flex — and three of them are lists of `row-default` rows.

Nobody has ever seen the eight stacked. v1 below is the measurement, and it is why this view came first: if the composition does not fit, then every other Calendar view is being designed on top of a problem.

Baseline

All eight sections, in the specified order, at the specified heights.

The measurement

Year view — “reduced density” is a promise about scale

`calendar.md` gives the Year the shortest sentence in the Calendar:

“Year view — 12 month grids at reduced density. Used for browsing, not acting.”

Reduced density is a promise about scale, and a month grid cannot be scaled down. Seven days is the phone's width divided by seven — 50px in the Month and 44px here, where 38px of each row goes to the month's name. That width is a floor rather than a setting, and it barely moves. The only thing there is room to shrink is a cell's height, and it does: from 115px in the Month to 9px here.

That matters because of what the Month decided. The Month's answer to “density” was position by time — a mark drawn where the event sits in its day. That encoding is entirely vertical. Squash the cell and it does not degrade gracefully, it disappears: a 30-minute event and a 3-hour one are both one pixel.

So the question is not how to fit twelve of these. It is: what survives the zoom?

“12 month grids at reduced density”, read literally — with the Month's own encoding carried down into them.

The measurement

Event detail — the container is a spec question

The four zoom levels are all reached the same way: from the mode, by pinch. Event detail is the first surface reached from inside a view, and the only one that joins other modules — attendees are People, linked notes are Notes, and the prep card is Tier 2. Its whole specification is one sentence:

“Event detail. Opened by tapping an event. Shows title, time, attendees (People), linked notes, prep card (via Tier 2 ‘Prep me’), and an Edit action for in-app events.”

Three other docs contradict or constrain that sentence, and the three readings do not agree:

  • surfaces.md calls Event detail a Screen. calendar.md's own example says it “opens as a sheet”. motion-vocabulary.md describes a shared-element list → detail transition where the row expands into the detail, which is a screen push. One of these is wrong.
  • tier-2-contextual.md: “Tier 2 is always user-initiated. There is no ambient Tier 2.” So the prep card cannot be on the view when it opens. The sentence lists a part that is absent by invariant.
  • The same doc's fallbacks say a Tier 2 result appears in the sheet, and gesture-vocabulary.md says the long-press menu “appears as a sheet”. With app-shell.md's at most one sheet at a time, those two facts settle the first question — and not in calendar.md's favour.
