Calendar lab

The Calendar is the app's default mode and the hub every other module attaches to — the surface where the Day becomes visible. Each view is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left, and the same three lines on the right — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks.

The fourth row is the recommended one: the synthesis, not a fifth option.

fixed: token set · palette · type scale · Lucide · row heights · motion rules · `calendar.md`'s eight sections

Day view — the composition, at last

`calendar.md` specifies the Day view as a fixed vertical composition of eight parts: day header, now line, timeline, habits due, metric log, day notes, top three, completed. Seven of those eight have a height that does not bend — the timeline is the only one that can flex — and three of them are lists of `row-default` rows.

Nobody has ever seen the eight stacked. v1 below is the measurement, and it is why this view came first: if the composition does not fit, then every other Calendar view is being designed on top of a problem.

Baseline

All eight sections, in the specified order, at the specified heights.

Top three, completed and notes collapse to "3 planned · 2 done · 1 note". Nothing is removed; the counts are doors.
Cost

Against rules
Two. `calendar.md` fixes the eight sections and their order, and a summary line is not a section. And the timeline is specified as events and scheduled tasks positioned by time — an unscheduled habit is neither.

Changed
Seven scrolling parts become two bands and an axis: an anytime band for the things with no time, then one axis holding events, a completion, and the now marker in their real order. A completion is drawn on the axis at the moment it happened, which is the one thing no other variant does.
Cost
The axis gets long, and the now marker has to stay findable — on a full day the line that says "you are here" is one of twenty. It also merges two kinds of object into one column, so "what happened" and "what is planned" read the same at a glance.
Against rules
Two. A completed task is not an event or a scheduled task, and `calendar.md` says the timeline holds those two. The anytime band is a ninth part the section list does not have.
Design taste

Delete what is reachable elsewhere. A hub should not duplicate its spokes.

Changed
Four sections removed: top three is the Tasks list with a ranking on it, completed is Tasks → filter Completed, day notes is Notes → Daily, and the metric row's label folds into "Due today". What is left is what only exists here.
Cost
The hub stops being a hub. "Top three" is the app's ranking for the day, set during shutdown — removing it from the Day view makes the ritual's output invisible where the day is read. And the completed section was added one commit ago precisely so the day's record closes the view.
Against rules
One: it deletes four sections `calendar.md` specifies. A lens may argue for that; it cannot decide it.
Recommended

Keep all eight. Change what "a fixed composition" means: an order, not a height.

Takes
Baseline for keeping every section and their order — each has a job and none is decoration. Impeccable for the realisation that the sections have wildly different weights and the heavy ones belong below the fold. Design taste for naming what is reachable elsewhere, which is what makes four sections harmless to put second.
Fixes
The composition becomes an order rather than a height, and the view scrolls under a header and a now line that do not. Of the six scrolling parts, five are above the fold — timeline, due row, metric, Daily Note, top three — and only the Completed list falls below, by 52px. That is the right part to lose: it is the record, read at the end of the day rather than scanned during it, and at 138px it was the section that broke the fit in the first place.
Still needs
Two spec edits this view cannot make by itself. calendar.md calls the Day view "a fixed vertical composition" — it has to become a fixed order with a height that scrolls. And doing-the-day.md calls the now line a persistent instrument that is always present; a scrolling day takes it off screen, so it has to be specified as not scrolling.
