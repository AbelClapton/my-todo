Calendar lab

The Calendar is the app's default mode and the hub every other module attaches to — the surface where the Day becomes visible. Each view is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left, and the same three lines on the right — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks.

The fourth row is the recommended one: the synthesis, not a fifth option.

fixed: token set · palette · type scale · Lucide · row heights · motion rules · `calendar.md`'s eight sections

Day view — the composition, at last

`calendar.md` specifies the Day view as a fixed vertical composition of eight parts: day header, now line, timeline, habits due, metric log, day notes, top three, completed. Seven of those eight have a height that does not bend — the timeline is the only one that can flex — and three of them are lists of `row-default` rows.

Nobody has ever seen the eight stacked. v1 below is the measurement, and it is why this view came first: if the composition does not fit, then every other Calendar view is being designed on top of a problem.

Baseline

All eight sections, in the specified order, at the specified heights.

The measurement

Week view — one sentence, and it is a hard one

`calendar.md` gives the Day view eight numbered parts, its token decisions, and a measurement. It gives the Week this, in full:

“Week view — 7 days side by side. Events only; tasks and habits collapse to counts.”

One sentence of layout. Everything else — the axis, its window, whether a title is ever drawn, what “collapse to counts” counts, where the now line goes, what a bare week looks like — is undecided. So this view is not being checked against a layout. It is being asked whether the sentence can be drawn at all.

It can. But not the way the Day view works, and the measurement below is why.

Baseline

“7 days side by side”, read literally: seven equal columns, every event in the column it falls in, with its title.

The measurement

Month view — the spec already answered the hard question

`calendar.md` gives the Month one sentence, and it is a better sentence than the Week's:

“Month view — a grid. Days show event density, not event titles. Tapping a day opens it.”

The Week spent a whole lab discovering that a proportional column cannot hold a title. The Month was written presuming it — “not event titles” — which means the hard question is already behind it. What is left is narrower and sharper: what is “density”, and can a 50px cell carry it?

Two things the sentence does not say, and both of them decide the grid: how many rows it has — which sets every cell's height, and therefore whether two months can be compared at all — and what a tap opens.

Baseline

“A grid. Days show event density.” A count is the plainest reading of density there is.
