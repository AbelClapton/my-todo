V4 puts every time fact in a chip, which means a row can carry several. But design-tokens.md is absolute about height: "Every list uses one of these four. No custom row heights." A cluster that wraps pushes a row past `row-rich`, so this block is a stress test rather than a demonstration.

measured at 390px — 1 chip: title 195px / cluster 97 · 2 chips: 144 / 148 · 3 chips + dot: 60 / 232

Nothing overflows and nothing clips, which is the problem: the row height is fixed, so the pressure shows up as title starvation instead. At three chips the title is down to a fifth of the row and wraps, while the secondary content takes 59% of it — and it still looks fine at a glance.

So V4 needs one rule it does not currently have. The cheapest is a cap of two chips, in a fixed priority order, with the rest living in the detail view. Two chips leave the title 144px, which is what a title needs; three do not. A fifth row height would also work and is worse — it is a new token, so an ADR, for a case the cap removes entirely.

Icons

sizes are tokens · the glyphs are assets

Two icon usages were documented and did not exist: icon-md 20 as the default in a list row, and icon-xl 32 for an empty state. Neither surface ever grew one. Both are settled now — the row's glyph is inline, at icon-xs, and the empty state's is nothing at all — and design-tokens.md records icon-xl as unused.

Lucide is adopted (0.469.0, ISC), and the glyphs below are its real icon data, inlined so the lab stays offline. Lucide's drawing rules are a 24px grid, a uniform 2px stroke, round caps and joins, and currentColor — which is why a glyph inherits its colour and can never introduce one. The app installs lucide-react; the shapes and the rules are the same.

One exception: the control glyphs in the screen mocks below — search, add, settings, back, overflow — are still Unicode stand-ins. They are named in components.md and an implementation draws the real ones; they stay stand-ins here rather than wrong assets, because a gear path I cannot reproduce exactly would be a fabricated Lucide glyph claiming to be one.

04 Labels: goal and metric the case where an icon is not decoration

Accent against the semantics

a hue-distinctness question, not a contrast one

The accent has to read as interactive while danger, success, and warning read as state. They rarely appear as the same kind of object, but they share a screen, and the accent is the only one of the four that is a choice. Red, green, and amber are taken, which is why every accent below is cool — and why teal is the tightest case.
