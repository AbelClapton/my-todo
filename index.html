Small Wins — design labs

Throwaway evaluation artifacts. Not the app, not the implementation pattern, not the component library. They exist so design decisions get made by looking at something measured rather than by arguing from prose — and they have earned their keep: every lab so far has found a defect in the spec it was drawing.

The labs are static files with no build step and no network. Open them from a checkout, or from the published URL, and they behave identically.

Settled: warm light with cool dark, teal accent, vivid semantics, the system type stack, footnote-sized metadata.

Calendar lab

The Calendar's views, one at a time. The Day view first — where the app lives, and where eight sections had never been seen stacked — then the Week, which the spec covered in a single sentence, the Month, whose single sentence at least knew titles were impossible, and the Year, whose sentence asked for something that cannot be done.

Settled: the Day view is an order rather than a height. The Week cannot be a chart and legible — proportionality is what costs the titles. The Month's word “density” resolves to position, not to a count: twenty-four days described by five numbers is not a description. The Year's twelve month grids fit — 504 cells on one phone — while the Month's encoding collapses to a single pixel inside them. And the Event detail is a screen rather than a sheet, which one documented rule settles and two docs disagreed about — a rule that then broke New event the same way, since two of its fields open an overlay of their own.

Habits lab

The first module outside the Calendar, and the first one with a single component. The habit row is four elements in 56px — and its checkbox carries four states, two of which are drawn as a ring.

Settled: the module's own invariant is “Skipping is not failing”, which is a claim about whether two colours can be told apart — and the chart's two greys are 1.17:1 apart. The minimum is the spec's equal and the row's 1px underline. Habits due today are drawn three times, and one of the three offers no Skip. And 18/28 is uncomputable: a habit ten days old cannot have been due 28 times.

Notes lab

The module that is two things at once — a home and an attachment every other module can have. Its one component is a row specified at 72px holding a title, a chip, two lines of preview and an edit stamp.

Settled: nothing here is a preference. The specified type sizes need 82px of text in a 72px row, so the row is 106px as drawn. Two docs describe how a note is born and they disagree — capture.md attaches it to today’s Day and ends “does not require the user to pick anything” — while notes.md opens a target picker first. note.edited carries the whole body every 5 seconds with no coalescing rule and no stated embedding trigger. And auto-linking makes a note’s text a function of today’s date, so the same bytes link a name in September and do not in December.

Protocols lab

The app’s defining module — “what make the app different” — and the only one with a lifecycle: five statuses, a time box, and a control period.

Settled: the card is 110px against a 96px token — and the reason is the chrome, not the text: four lines need 78px and leave 18px to spare, while the padding, gaps and borders need 32. row-hero’s only real user is this card, so the token was sized for it and is still short. The other defects are silences. M = 42 — the sum of the baseline and the duration — is on every card and defined in no doc. The review date is displayed, fired on by two docs, and computed in none. The abandon threshold counts active days, so abandoning at the end of baseline discards 13 metric entries and reports nothing. And “not scored” is a state habits.md has no way to draw, because it never mentions baseline. The proposal sheet is the third surface the one-sheet rule decides — after the Event detail and New event.

People lab

The connective layer — “People is not a CRM. It is a lightweight entity that connects the other modules.” Its first invariant is stronger: a Person is a layer, not an atom.

Settled: the row is 3px over its token — the smallest overrun of the three labs that found one, against the note row’s 34px and the protocol card’s 14px. The overrun shrinks with the row’s complexity and the constant is that the chrome is never in the arithmetic. Beyond that the defects are silences: “involving them” is undefined — one hop for a task, two for a note — and the doc’s own examples use both readings of the same sentence. The + creates a Person that “does not exist on its own”. Contacts import writes 304 records on one tap and needs 304 archives to undo. And resurfacing has no population cap and no ordering, where the captures surface next door has “top 5 by age” — with “worth maintaining” defaulting to on for everyone.

Review lab

The weekly review — “the highest-value AI output in the app… the moment the app becomes something other than a tracker.” Two paragraphs, no gamification, and a required citation of its sources and their freshness.

Settled: the module is well specified — the nudge, the queue merge and the tone rules all check out against the attention budget, down to the urgency and TTL. The defects are inside the document. Its own example breaks three of its own rules: all four numbers in its Sources line contradict the paragraph above them, its second paragraph prescribes an action the content rules forbid, and its date window is Wed–Tue against a Sunday rule — already copied into retrieval.md. And the freshness clause (“Calendar as of 2 hours ago”) is true once and wrong by exactly the elapsed time forever after, in the doc that requires the note stay readable.

Settings lab

The last module, and the app’s longest list by an order of magnitude — 49 rows across 12 groups, a surface with no mode entry, reached from a palette or a gear.

Settled: 49 rows at row-compact, plus 12 headers and 11 group gaps, is 2,796px — 4 screens on the phone every other lab measured, with no search while the command palette is the documented entry point. Reset all settings writes 41 entries in one batch with no grouping ID and a 5-second undo, and 4 of those keys are integrations — resetting calendar reaches what Revoke calendar access does under confirmation. The surface opens in 2 containers depending on the door, the 4th surface a still-unwritten rule has decided. And 1 of the union’s 28 keys — integration.weather.enabled — has no row at all.

How to read a lab

Each screen is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left and everything that explains it on the right. Every row carries the same three lines — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks. "None" is a finding too, so no row leaves that cell empty.

The last row is the recommended one. It is not another option: it takes what the others agreed on, keeps the moves that survived their costs, and states what it still needs.

One constraint runs through all of it. The token set is closed, so no lens can move type or colour, and a distinctive visual identity is unavailable by construction. That is the token set working as intended — and it is why every difference worth arguing about is composition rather than style.
