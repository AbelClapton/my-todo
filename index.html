Small Wins — design labs

Throwaway evaluation artifacts. Not the app, not the implementation pattern, not the component library. They exist so design decisions get made by looking at something measured rather than by arguing from prose — and they have earned their keep: every lab so far has found a defect in the spec it was drawing.

The labs are static files with no build step and no network. Open them from a checkout, or from the published URL, and they behave identically.

Calendar lab

The Calendar's views, one at a time. The Day view first — where the app lives, and where eight sections had never been seen stacked — then the Week, which the spec covered in a single sentence, the Month, whose single sentence at least knew titles were impossible, and the Year, whose sentence asked for something that cannot be done.

Settled: the Day view is an order rather than a height. The Week cannot be a chart and legible — proportionality is what costs the titles. The Month's word “density” resolves to position, not to a count: twenty-four days described by five numbers is not a description. The Year's twelve month grids fit — 504 cells on one phone — while the Month's encoding collapses to a single pixel inside them. And the Event detail is a screen rather than a sheet, which one documented rule settles and two docs disagreed about — a rule that then broke New event the same way, since two of its fields open an overlay of their own.

How to read a lab

Each screen is a table of rows: one per variation, markup on the left and everything that explains it on the right. Every row carries the same three lines — what it changed, what it cost, and which of the app's own rules it breaks. "None" is a finding too, so no row leaves that cell empty.

The last row is the recommended one. It is not another option: it takes what the others agreed on, keeps the moves that survived their costs, and states what it still needs.

One constraint runs through all of it. The token set is closed, so no lens can move type or colour, and a distinctive visual identity is unavailable by construction. That is the token set working as intended — and it is why every difference worth arguing about is composition rather than style.
